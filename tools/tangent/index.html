01

Grab the point

Drag the orange point along the curve. The orange line only touches the curve there: that's the tangent. Its slope is the derivative f'(x).

scroll to see why it works
02

Zoom in until it's straight

Keep scrolling and the loupe zooms in on the point. Somewhere around ×20, the curve stops looking curved: up close, every smooth curve is a straight line.

The derivative is simply the slope of that line.

03

Squeeze the secant

Take a second point a distance h further along. The line through both is a secant, and its slope is rise over run.

\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}

Scroll, and h shrinks towards 0. The secant turns into the tangent, and its slope lands on the derivative:

f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}
04

Slope becomes a graph

Now the point sweeps from left to right. At every x, we take the slope of the tangent and plot it as a height underneath.

The orange curve the pen draws is a brand new function: f'.

05

Read f from f′

Where f' is above the axis, f goes up. Where it's below, f goes down.

Where f' crosses zero, f has a top or a bottom. Where f' turns around, f changes its bend: an inflection point.

This is exactly what exam questions about the graph of f' test.
